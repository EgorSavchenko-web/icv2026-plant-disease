\begin{frame}{Which transfer strategy survives leaving the laboratory?}

\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.56\textwidth}
PlantVillage: 38 plant diseases, photographed in a lab --- one leaf, grey background, even light.
A pretrained ResNet-50 saturates on it.

\vspace{6pt}
So we ask a harder question. Same network, \alert{one variable}: which parameters get gradients.

\vspace{6pt}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textcolor{blue!55!black}{\textbf{Linear probe}} --- backbone frozen, 78\,k parameters
  \item \textcolor{orange!75!black}{\textbf{Fine-tuned}} --- all 23.6\,M parameters
\end{itemize}

\vspace{6pt}
Then we photograph the test set with a \textbf{field robot} instead: motion blur, low light with
sensor noise, JPEG for the radio link.

\vspace{4pt}
Three severities, three seeds, no retraining.
\end{column}

\begin{frame}{Fine-tuning wins --- until the noise starts}

\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.92\linewidth]{degradation_accuracy.png}

\vspace{6pt}
\raggedright
\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
Clean data, seed 42: \textbf{3 errors against 34}.\\[4pt]
Blur and JPEG: fine-tuning ahead everywhere, by up to 11 points.
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
Sensor noise: \alert{the order reverses}, by 24 points at severity 2.\\[4pt]
Every seed agrees --- the best fine-tuned run still loses to the worst probe.
\end{column}
\end{columns}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- 3
\begin{frame}{Why, what breaks, and what to take away}

\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.50\textwidth}
\textbf{A control narrows the cause.}\\[3pt]
The third panel is pure Gaussian noise, same brightness, no gain. It reproduces the reversal, one
severity earlier.

\vspace{6pt}
So noise alone is enough. \emph{Why} it hurts fine-tuning --- filters tuned to lab texture --- is
still a hypothesis, not a result.

\vspace{14pt}
\textbf{The failure has a shape.}\\[3pt]
Under noise the fine-tuned model sends \alert{61--76\,\% of its errors into one class} ---
Septoria leaf spot, which looks like dark speckle. The probe drifts there too, far less.
\end{column}

\begin{column}{0.46\textwidth}
\textbf{And it stops admitting doubt.}\\[3pt]
Clean: fine-tuning is the better calibrated. Low light, severity 2: ECE \alert{0.26--0.42 against
0.17--0.20}, three seeds.

\vspace{14pt}
\textbf{Take away.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Fine-tune by default.
  \item Robustness is not one number --- it flips with the \emph{kind} of degradation.
  \item Report calibration, not only accuracy.
\end{itemize}
\end{column}
\end{columns}
\end{frame}
